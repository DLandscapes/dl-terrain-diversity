    Selection —
      Rows combine top to bottom, starting from nothing: + adds, − takes away, ∩ keeps only what is also inside. So steep ground, but not the high part of it, and only within the site boundary is three rows in that order — and the order is the meaning, which is why rows move rather than sort. A saved layer is frozen: it holds the cells you accepted rather than re-reading the ground under you mid-cut. One read off the surface says so and offers to re-read when the ground moves; a polygon never does, because geometry does not move when the ground is cut.

        Select the .shp, and the .prj with it if you have one — nothing is reprojected, so a mismatched CRS is named rather than silently assumed. One feature becomes one region, holes included. DXF is not read yet; see the note in the design document.
        By attribute
        A selection is where the modifiers are allowed to act; an attribute is one way of building one. It reads the CURRENT surface, so re-applying it after an edit selects different cells — "where it is steep" means where it is steep now. Cells with no answer (wetness on level ground, aspect on a flat cell) are excluded, never counted as zero.

        Benches the whole tile twice at each target tread — once with one interval and one datum, once patch by landform patch on each patch's own slope and floor — with the uniform interval searched so both schemes move the same volume. Reads the surface as it stands and changes nothing.
        Curve
        1. Press Curve above — the view locks to plan, because a line traced in a tilted view lands somewhere other than where it looked.
        2. Click along the ground to place points. Two make a line, more give it bends. There is nothing to finish — the line is whatever you have clicked so far, and it keeps extending until you cut it.
        3. Choose a cross-section, and what the line does with height — level holds water, grade moves it, follow keeps a constant depth below the ground.
        4. The sentence below prices it before anything moves. Cut the curve commits it; Ctrl+Z takes it back, and clear the line throws the line away without cutting.
        The edge condition uses the same two batter angles as plan levelling — a cutting stands at its own stability, an embankment ravels to its repose angle. A graded channel conveys water; this tool states the gradient and the volume, never a speed.

        Infiltration is one coefficient per substrate class, not soil physics — a stated design assumption, and zero where no substrate has been specified. Sub-basins inside one hollow are not resolved: the patch's 929 hollows average 7 litres each, so there is no interior to resolve.
        Species
        A habitat-suitability instrument, not a prediction. Each species has a tolerance range per axis — moisture, energy, substrate, shelter, landform — combined so that the worst axis governs. The envelopes are stated assumptions, open to argument; nothing here is fitted to occurrence data and no uncertainty is carried. It answers whether a gesture widens or narrows the range of conditions on the site, not which plant will grow.

          Export
          Shapefile (.shp/.shx/.dbf/.prj, EPSG:25833) and GeoJSON of the same rings, zipped. Attributes: id, name, level_m — the datum each region was told to reach, which makes the set a specification rather than a tracing.
          The section SVG is vector at a stated scale, existing ground dashed and proposed solid, cut and fill hatched in opposite directions so they survive greyscale. Areas are m² on the section, not volumes: the ledger is the only place a volume comes from.
          Isopach: equal depth of cut (dashed) and fill (solid), the limit of works heavy. Slope classes: gradient bands as ratios, denser hatch is steeper. Drainage: channels, divides, standing water and ranked outfalls at a stated event. Chainage sections: cross-sections at even stations along the guide, looking along it, areas per section.
          GeoTIFF keeps EPSG:25833 · OBJ is local, Z-up, true elevations · the figure carries its legend and units · Everything bundles the terrain, every layer's values and every figure, with a README
